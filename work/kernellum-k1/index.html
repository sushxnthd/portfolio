Kernellum / K1

K1 asks a deliberately harder question than simulator-only architecture search: do predicted gains survive synthesis, place-and-route and physical-design variability?

The experiment

Nine initial architectures were implemented on a Lattice ECP5 target. Those final-route observations trained a surrogate that selected four new architectures. An equal-budget random arm selected four others. All eight new designs routed successfully.

Beyond one winner

A later preregistered confirmation treated route-seed variability as part of the problem instead of averaging it away. A two-candidate portfolio was evaluated across 72 new final-route implementations. All 13 frozen criteria passed; the portfolio beat the best broadcast-only implementation in 36 / 36 unseen deployment cases, with 23.56% mean modeled workload-latency improvement and 1.44% mean regret to the full routed oracle.

What is still missing

This is not fabricated silicon and it is not board-measured latency, power or energy. K2 is board-ready but remains physically unvalidated until the generated bitstreams run on the real board and the preregistered 100-trial-per-architecture gate passes.

Kernellum also preserves negative results: a separate adaptive experiment-selection component failed its improvement gate against space-filling sampling instead of being reframed as a success.
