Theorica

Why it exists

Research agents can generate hypotheses, but weak controls and shallow evaluation make “discovery” easy to overclaim. THEORICA is built around experimental progress: hypotheses, informative experiments, strong baselines, falsification and the next test.

The problem

Given only queried operation values, can a learner reconstruct the full operation table without being told the algebra's family? The representation uses a small permutation base when the compact-action conditions hold; a scorer evaluates eligibility using the hidden full table only after acquisition.

The initial representation could agree on its current base even when a true translation was absent. Rather than immediately abstain on a validation counterexample, v1.2 queries the missing translation, expands the group and retries. Corpus, split, budgets and frozen gates remained unchanged.

What survived scrutiny

An adversarial prior-art audit rejected broad novelty claims about algebra learning, property testing and active selection. The narrower integration hypothesis remains plausible, not a certified first. Fixed validation queries were effectively exhaustive for tiny loop and quandle instances; their exactness does not establish subquadratic scaling.

Attribution and next test

The project citation names Sushanth Dasari as author of THEORICA. The translation-base branch uses public GAP SmallGrp and RightQuasigroups catalogues. A next scientific test is to measure scaling on larger independently held-out instances, where exhaustive validation no longer conceals acquisition costs.
